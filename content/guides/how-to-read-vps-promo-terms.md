<section class="page-hero"><p class="eyebrow">BUYING GUIDE</p><h1>How to read VPS promotion terms before checkout</h1><p class="lede">Separate the introductory period, contract commitment and later charges before treating an offer as a saving.</p></section><article class="prose"><h2>Check three dates: promotion, contract and renewal</h2>
<p>A promotion may last three months while the contract lasts a year. Renewal terms decide what you pay after that agreement ends. Write down all three periods before comparing prices.</p>
<p>At this source review, the IONOS VPS page pairs its introductory offer with a three-month promotion and a one-year term. This is an example of why the promotion period and commitment must be read separately, not a promise about tomorrow's checkout. Our current offer pages retain the source retrieval time and purchase conditions. Reopen the official page before paying.</p>
<h2>Build a charge worksheet</h2>
<p>Copy the details from the actual checkout for your chosen plan and account region. Keep the currency, billing period and tax treatment beside each amount. Mark unknown amounts as unknown; an empty field is a reason to investigate, not permission to substitute the introductory price.</p>

<h2>Calculate the cost over the same period</h2>
<p>For a fixed-term comparison, use: total commitment cost = introductory rate × introductory months + later rate × remaining months + mandatory fees + selected extras + applicable tax. Every input must come from the relevant provider's checkout or agreement. If later charges or tax are unknown, do not claim a total or savings percentage. Usage-based services require an additional workload estimate and a separately identified rate schedule.</p>
<p>The cheapest advertised month can therefore differ from the cheapest plan over your actual project lifetime. Compare the same period and the same required features. A prepaid annual commitment and a flexible server are not interchangeable just because both display a monthly amount. Use the amount you will actually pay, not the crossed-out reference price.</p>
<h2>Read network language carefully</h2>
<p>Traffic allowance describes a quantity of transferred data; bandwidth describes a rate. Neither, on its own, tells you the application's response time. When a page says unlimited traffic, check the terms that accompany that exact product. Ask about acceptable use, throttling, excluded destinations and any plan-specific restrictions. Do not import conditions from a different provider or region.</p>
<h2>Check eligibility and cancellation before ordering</h2>
<p>A promotion may depend on the account, location, payment term or product variation. Follow the official purchase path and read the final summary. Do not buy through an unofficial reseller to obtain an unverified coupon. If a trial or refund policy is relevant to your decision, check what it covers and whether add-ons have different conditions. Cancellation, refund and deletion are separate actions; none should be assumed to imply the others.</p>
<p>Record the agreement you accepted privately, with an order reference, and set your own review reminder before any relevant deadline. Do not post invoices, bank details or account identifiers in a public issue. After cancellation, check whether disks, snapshots or other separately billed resources remain. The provider's billing documentation is the authority for what continues to incur charges.</p>
