<h2>The short answer</h2><p>For most classroom prep, three free options cover the work: MagicSchool AI for lesson plans, worksheets and rubrics; Canva for Education for slides, posters and worksheets; and NotebookLM for turning your own documents into study resources. Pick one as your daily planning tool. Use the second when the first hits a limit. Claim an educator plan only after reading the eligibility terms on the provider's own page.</p><p>Do not start by paying. Pay only when a free tier blocks real prep work more than once in a week, and only after you have checked what the paid tier adds on the official pricing page.</p>
<h2>Three verified free options</h2><p>Each entry below states what the provider's own page or a checked directory showed on 2026-10-23. Limits and prices change; confirm on the linked page before you rely on them.</p>
<h3>1. MagicSchool AI</h3><p>MagicSchool's free plan includes its teacher tools for planning, assessment, differentiation and communication, with daily generation limits; the paid Plus plan removes those limits. <a href="https://www.magicschool.ai/" rel="noopener">Official site</a>.</p><p>Use it for: lesson plan scaffolds, worksheet drafts, rubric criteria, exit tickets. Expect the daily generation cap to be the binding limit in a heavy planning week, not the number of tools.</p>
<h3>2. Canva for Education</h3><p>Canva for Education is free for eligible K-12 educators after verification, and includes classroom design tools and AI-assisted writing for instructional materials. <a href="https://www.canva.com/education/" rel="noopener">Official education page</a>.</p><p>Use it for: slides, posters, worksheets and other visual classroom materials. The eligibility check is the gate: confirm your school and role qualify before building a term's materials inside it.</p>
<h3>3. NotebookLM</h3><p>NotebookLM is available free and works from documents you supply, turning your own materials into summaries and study resources. <a href="https://notebooklm.google/" rel="noopener">Official site</a>.</p><p>Use it for: turning a chapter, handout or set of notes into a study guide, FAQ or quiz draft. Because it works from your sources, it is the least likely of the three to invent content — but still check every output before handing it to students.</p>

<h2>Five checks before you rely on any &ldquo;free&rdquo; tool</h2>
<p><strong>1. Plan or trial?</strong> A free plan continues; a free trial ends. Trials that ask for a card up front will charge you if you forget to cancel.</p>
<p><strong>2. What is capped?</strong> Generations per day, documents per month, or features behind a paywall are three different limits. Find the one that binds your heaviest week.</p>
<p><strong>3. Does it need educator verification?</strong> Some education offers require a verified school email or role check. Start that process before you need the tool, not the night before class.</p>
<p><strong>4. What happens to student data?</strong> Never paste student names, grades or identifying details into any AI tool. Describe students in general terms — grade level, learning needs, performance level — and check the provider's privacy page before any classroom use.</p>
<p><strong>5. What changes at renewal?</strong> Student and educator offers can change each school year. Re-check the pricing page at the start of every term.</p>
<h2>A one-week trial plan</h2><p>Pick one recurring task — for example, next week's lesson plans. Use one tool for that task all week and count how many times you hit a limit. If you hit a limit more than once, that tool's free tier does not fit that task; try the next one on the list. If you hit no limits, you have a working stack and no reason to pay yet.</p>
<h2>Questions teachers ask</h2>
<p><strong>Can students use the same tools?</strong> Teacher access and student access are different offers. Check the provider's education page for student terms separately; do not assume a teacher plan covers a class.</p>
<p><strong>Is one general assistant enough?</strong> For short drafts and explanations, yes. Purpose-built teacher tools earn their place on structured tasks — rubrics, differentiated readings, feedback comments — where a general prompt needs rewriting every time.</p>
<p><strong>Should I check AI output before sharing it?</strong> Always. AI drafts can contain errors, outdated facts and invented references. Review every worksheet, rubric and handout against your lesson before it reaches students.</p>
