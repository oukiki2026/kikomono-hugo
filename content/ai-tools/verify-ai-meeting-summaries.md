<section class="page-hero"><p class="eyebrow">AI WORKFLOW GUIDE</p><h1>Verify AI meeting summaries before sharing them</h1><p class="lede">Check decisions, owners, deadlines and recording access before an AI summary becomes a team record.</p></section><article class="prose"><h2>Verify commitments, not just grammar</h2>
<p>Before sharing an AI meeting summary, compare each decision, task owner and deadline with the recording or a participant's confirmation. A tidy layout can still hide a wrong decision. Keep unresolved questions visible rather than turning them into decisions for the sake of a tidy recap.</p>
<h2>Separate suggestions from decisions</h2>
<p>Consider an illustrative meeting where someone says “Could we launch on Friday?” and another person says “We need the security review first.” A summary saying “The team agreed to launch Friday” would discard the condition. The exchange is an invented example. A better record labels Friday as proposed and the security review as a prerequisite until participants confirm a commitment.</p>
<h2>Put the evidence beside each decision</h2>
<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th>Field</th><th>Review question</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Decision</td><td>Was it accepted, proposed or still disputed?</td></tr>
<tr><td>Owner</td><td>Did this person accept the task?</td></tr>
<tr><td>Deadline</td><td>Is the date explicit, and are timezone or prerequisites relevant?</td></tr>
<tr><td>Evidence</td><td>Can a reviewer locate the supporting passage?</td></tr>
<tr><td>Open issue</td><td>What still needs confirmation?</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<p>If you cannot find supporting evidence for an action item, mark it for confirmation instead of sending it as an instruction. Check an important decision with its owner when the audio is ambiguous. AI output should not create an obligation that participants did not accept.</p>
<h2>Check recording access before the meeting</h2>
<p>Agree on whether the meeting may be recorded and processed with the selected service. Review your organization's policy, the provider's retention and deletion controls, and who can access transcripts or shared links. Use a nonsensitive sample for evaluation. Do not upload customer secrets, credentials or identity documents merely to test a note-taking tool.</p>
<p>Confirm the scope of a shared link before distributing it. A summary intended for colleagues may contain details that should not appear in a public link. Share the minimum necessary version, and establish who can remove it. If a workflow cannot meet your confidentiality needs, use an approved alternative rather than relying on a general marketing security claim.</p>
<h2>Make corrections traceable</h2>
<p>Keep the original output and a corrected version with a reviewer and review date. If a correction changes a decision already sent to recipients, issue an explicit correction rather than silently replacing the file. Preserve only what your retention policy permits; keeping every recording forever creates its own access burden.</p>
<h2>Read the file your team will receive</h2>
<p>Test the path from recording to the final team document. Ask whether a recipient can distinguish confirmed decisions from open issues without asking you to explain the summary.
