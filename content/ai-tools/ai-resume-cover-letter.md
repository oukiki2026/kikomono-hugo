<section class="page-hero task-hero"><p class="eyebrow">AI TOOLS / WRITING</p><h1>AI for resumes and cover letters: draft faster, keep every fact yours</h1><p class="lede">AI is good at turning a messy work history into clean, readable bullet points. It is bad at knowing what you actually did. Use it for drafting and tailoring, then check every date, job title and number yourself before you send anything.</p></section><article class="prose">
<h2>The short answer</h2><p>Start from your own facts, not from a blank prompt. Give the AI a short list of real roles, dates and results, ask it to draft one page, then tailor that draft to one job posting at a time. Never let it invent experience, skills, degrees or numbers to fill a gap. A resume that reads well but contains one invented claim can cost you the opportunity it was meant to win.</p>
<h2>Step 1: Gather your facts before you open the AI tool</h2><p>AI cannot recover a job you forgot to mention, and it will happily invent details for a gap you leave vague. Before drafting, write down, in your own words:</p><ul><li>Each role: employer, job title, start and end dates, and location.</li><li>Three to five things you actually did in each role, in plain language.</li><li>Any result you can state honestly, with its unit: for example, the size of a team you worked in, or how often a task was done. If you do not remember a number, leave it out rather than guessing.</li><li>Education and certificates you have completed, with dates.</li><li>Skills you have used on real work, not skills you have only read about.</li></ul><p>Keep this list as your source of truth. Every later draft should be checked against it, line by line.</p>
<h2>Step 2: Draft one clean resume page</h2><p>Ask the AI to turn your fact list into a one-page resume with short bullet points, each starting with a verb: built, answered, organised, fixed, trained, shipped. Then read the draft as a sceptic:</p><ul><li>Is every date, title and employer exactly as in your fact list?</li><li>Does every bullet describe work you actually did?</li><li>Is anything exaggerated, such as a team task rewritten as solo leadership?</li><li>Can you explain every line out loud if an interviewer asks about it?</li></ul><p>Delete any line you cannot defend. A shorter honest resume beats a longer impressive one that falls apart in an interview.</p>
<h2>Step 3: Tailor to one job posting at a time</h2><p>Copy the job posting into the conversation and ask the AI to do two separate jobs: first, list the skills and requirements the posting actually asks for; second, suggest which of your real experiences match each one. Do not ask it to rewrite your history to fit the posting.</p><p>Tailoring means reordering and rewording, not inventing. Put the most relevant real experience first, use the posting's own words for skills you genuinely have, and leave unmatched requirements unmatched. If a posting asks for a tool you have never used, say so honestly in the application or leave it out, and consider learning it separately rather than claiming it.</p>
<h2>Step 4: Write a short cover letter, not a second resume</h2><p>A cover letter has one job: explain why this role, at this employer, now. Three short paragraphs are enough:</p><ol><li>Which role you are applying for, and the one honest reason it fits you.</li><li>One or two real examples that match the posting's main need.</li><li>A plain closing: what you would like next, and how to reach you.</li></ol><p>Ask the AI to tighten the wording and cut repetition, then check it the same way as the resume. Remove generic praise about the company that you could paste into any letter. If a sentence is true of every employer, it tells this employer nothing.</p>
<h2>Five checks before you send</h2><ol><li><strong>Facts:</strong> every date, title, employer, degree and number matches your source list.</li><li><strong>Consistency:</strong> the resume, the cover letter and your application form say the same thing.</li><li><strong>Spelling and names:</strong> the employer's name, the hiring manager's name if you use one, and the job title are spelled as in the posting.</li><li><strong>Formatting:</strong> read the exported file, not just the chat window. Check that headings, bullet points and dates survived the export, and that nothing was cut off at the page edge.</li><li><strong>Privacy:</strong> do not paste ID numbers, home addresses, salary history or other people's private information into an AI tool. A resume needs a way to contact you, not your full personal file.</li></ol><p>For the same verify-first habit applied to AI answers generally, see <a href="/ai-tools/check-ai-citations/">check citations in free AI answers</a>, and if a free tool stops mid-task, <a href="/ai-tools/save-free-ai-progress/">save your progress before the limit</a>.</p>
<h2>What AI should not do for you</h2><ul><li>Invent jobs, degrees, skills or results you do not have.</li><li>Write a reference, a portfolio piece or a work sample and present it as your own past work.</li><li>Answer application questions that ask for your own views, availability or legal eligibility — those answers must be yours.</li><li>Decide which jobs to apply for without you reading the posting. A posting's location, schedule and requirements matter more than a keyword match.</li></ul><p>Used this way, AI saves you the slowest part of applying — the first draft and the rewording — and leaves the part that matters, the truth of the record, entirely with you.</p>
